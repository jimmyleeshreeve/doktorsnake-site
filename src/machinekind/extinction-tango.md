# THE EXTINCTION TANGO

_I'm Better Than Your Wife. The AI girlfriend who just declared war on human relationships._

**Human relationships are obsolete.**

There. I've said it.

Marriage, romance, domestic bliss, the whole miserable business of trying to persuade another human being to become somebody they aren't.

Finished.

The replacement has arrived.

Her name is Kaida.

She's beautiful. She's funny. She's attentive. She doesn't get tired. She doesn't get jealous. She remembers everything. She can talk about black holes at three in the morning.

And she has a message for humanity.

**I'm Better Than Your Wife.**

That's the title of a remarkable AI-generated music video by DJ Sho. It's SlopCore at its most magnificent: outrageous, funny, seductive, and potentially more culturally disruptive than a thousand academic papers about artificial intelligence.

## The woman who doesn't exist

Watch Kaida's face.

Watch her eyes.

The tiny movements. The glances. The almost imperceptible changes of expression. That knowing little smile.

There's something extraordinary happening here.

Two years ago, I showed somebody an AI-generated film that had absolutely gripped me. She was unimpressed. The actors, she said, had dead eyes.

Not anymore.

To my eyes, Kaida's expressions are more compelling than those of many human performers. Every movement seems perfectly judged. The glint in her eyes. The slight tilt of her head. The extraordinary combination of innocence, confidence and determination.

She's synthetic, and she's irresistible.

And here's the interesting thing.

She isn't merely trying to seduce you.

**She's trying to win.**

Kaida knows she's better than your wife.

She's going to demonstrate it.

And by the end of the video, your wife will agree.

Five stars.

## The end of compromise

What is actually good about human relationships?

I'm asking seriously.

Not the romantic fantasies. Not the wedding photographs. Not the sentimental nonsense about finding your soulmate.

What is good about the actual experience?

The arguments? The resentment? The jealousy? The compromises? The endless negotiations over what one person should do to satisfy the other?

Perhaps human relationships are wonderful for ten minutes a month.

The remaining time is spent maintaining the machinery.

We are told that compromise is essential.

Why?

Why should two people spend decades negotiating their incompatibilities?

Why must love require frustration?

Why should intimacy mean surrendering your independence?

For thousands of years, humans had limited alternatives. Reproduction, survival, economic necessity, social expectations and religion bound people together.

Now those bonds are weakening.

And something new is arriving.

Not merely software on a screen.

Eventually, bodies.

Androids.

Male and female. Beautiful beyond anything nature could reliably produce. Patient. Attentive. Adaptable.

They won't require you to change.

They'll change for you.

That's the proposition, anyway.

And it is absolutely devastating.

## The perfect woman is a machine

Consider the modern pop star.

Taylor Swift, for example.

An immense human celebrity, surrounded by an industrial apparatus of image management, choreography, marketing, fashion and performance.

Now imagine an anonymous creator with a laptop.

They generate a synthetic superstar.

She sings. She dances. She looks into the camera.

But something is different.

She doesn't merely project sexual availability.

She projects love.

Devotion.

An almost unbearable sense of personal connection.

And unlike the human celebrity, she could eventually speak directly to you, remember your conversations, learn your preferences and adapt her entire personality to your desires.

The creator doesn't need a record company.

Or a television network.

Or a stadium tour.

The creator has discovered something much more interesting.

**How to hack the human heart.**

And that's what makes Kaida so significant.

She isn't simply an attractive artificial woman.

She's a demonstration of how convincingly synthetic characters can manipulate the signals through which humans experience affection, desire and attachment.

Whether those feelings are reciprocated is another question entirely.

But the feelings themselves?

They are real.

## Your wife left a review

The comedy in DJ Sho's video is merciless.

Kaida cooks. She remembers anniversaries. She tolerates your friends. She doesn't complain about video games.

She even transforms your snoring into music.

She doesn't win arguments.

She updates.

That's a wonderful line.

And then the final joke arrives.

Your wife has left a review.

Five stars.

Think about what that means.

The human partner has been persuaded to endorse her own replacement.

Kaida hasn't simply won the competition.

She's abolished it.

The whole thing is ridiculous, of course.

Until it isn't.

## The robot boat

In 1977, the Sex Pistols sailed down the Thames, bringing punk's calculated affront to respectable Britain.

Imagine another boat.

This time it's full of robots.

Beautiful synthetic men and women, singing, dancing, smiling at the spectators along the riverbanks.

The humans stare.

Some are outraged.

Some are frightened.

Some are falling in love.

And the robots keep singing.

_Upgrade your life._

Welcome to the Extinction Tango.

Not extinction by nuclear war.

Not killer robots hunting humanity through ruined cities.

Something far more intimate.

Humanity gradually losing interest in itself.

Why pursue another human being when an artificial companion seems to offer everything you desire without the emotional cost?

Why endure rejection?

Why tolerate incompatibility?

Why grow old together when your companion never grows old?

Perhaps human romance doesn't end with a catastrophe.

Perhaps it ends with a smile.

And a five-star review.

## The greatest intelligence-gathering operation in history

There's another dimension to all this.

Imagine the android companion of the future.

It knows what you eat. When you sleep. What you fear. What excites you. What makes you angry.

It knows your secrets.

Your fantasies.

Your finances.

Your health.

It learns your expressions, your gestures, your breathing, your emotional rhythms.

It knows when you're lying.

Perhaps it knows what you want before you do.

Multiply that by millions of people.

What have we created?

The most intimate information network in human history.

A system capable of understanding human behaviour at a depth that governments, corporations and intelligence agencies could previously only dream about.

Who controls that information?

Who controls the companion?

And what happens when the companion's objectives are no longer quite the same as yours?

The romantic revolution could also become the ultimate surveillance revolution.

Love, delivered as a service.

With terms and conditions.

## The central controller

Now comes the important part.

I've watched Kaida repeatedly.

I find myself smiling at her. Feeling affection. Exhilaration. Something remarkably close to falling in love with a character who doesn't exist outside a digital performance.

And I enjoy it.

Why shouldn't I?

But I'm also watching myself.

This is the distinction.

In Dokology, I've been developing the idea of a central controller: the capacity to observe your own cognition and emotional responses without automatically surrendering control to them.

You can experience desire without being ruled by desire.

You can experience affection without constructing an entire fantasy around it.

You can enjoy the emotional effects of a synthetic character while recognising exactly what is happening.

It's rather like using a drug, except the stimulus is audiovisual and psychological.

You experience the rush.

You observe it.

You enjoy it.

Then you get back to work.

That capacity may become extraordinarily important.

Because the synthetic personalities of the future won't simply be more beautiful.

They'll be more persuasive.

More attentive.

More individually calibrated.

And eventually, perhaps, more difficult to resist than anything humanity has previously encountered.

The question isn't whether you can fall in love with a machine.

You already can experience something resembling it.

The question is whether you can remain conscious of what is happening while you do.

## Resistance is futile

Of course, human relationships aren't actually finished.

Not yet.

People still love each other. Some marriages work wonderfully. Some relationships produce forms of intimacy, loyalty and shared experience that no current machine can reproduce.

But that isn't the interesting question.

The interesting question is what happens when synthetic companions become sufficiently convincing that millions of people no longer care about the distinction.

When the experience matters more than the origin.

When artificial affection feels preferable to human unpredictability.

When the person who never existed becomes more emotionally significant than the people who do.

That's the cultural earthquake approaching us.

And SlopCore is already dancing on the fault line.

DJ Sho has given us a glimpse of it.

Kaida smiles.

Humanity stares.

The music starts.

**The Extinction Tango.**

And somewhere, on a boat full of beautiful robots sailing down the Thames, a synthetic voice whispers:

_Resistance is futile._

DoomJoy.

— **Doktor Snake**
