# THE KEYS TO THE TAROT, PART FIVE — THE GIRL WHO WASN'T BORN YET

**She was carrying a battered laptop under one arm. In her other hand was a small electronic device with wires hanging from it.**

<img class="about-hero" src="/assets/img/whiskers-moonlit-tarot.jpg" alt="Whiskers and his moonlit tarot" loading="lazy">

The first thing I noticed was the boots.

Heavy black things, scuffed at the toes, with thick soles that made a considerable noise on Len's kitchen floor.

The second thing was the girl wearing them.

She had black hair sticking up in all directions, heavy eyeliner, a pale face, and an oversized hoodie with some sort of gothic design running down the sleeves. Underneath was a black lace top. She looked about twenty-three, perhaps younger.

She was carrying a battered laptop under one arm. In her other hand was a small electronic device with wires hanging from it.

She stopped in the doorway.

"Oh," she said. "People."

I looked at Len.

"Who the fuck are you?" I asked her. Then turned back to him. "She'll probably want to stay here too. Len, mate, is your place a doss house or what?"

Len looked sheepish.

"I don't normally get any visitors. Only when I need a plumber to fix my pipes, and he doesn't hang about for a chat."

"Well, you've certainly made up for it today."

The girl stepped into the kitchen.

"Are you Dok?"

"Depends who's asking."

"Mara."

I waited.

"Mara Voss."

"Right. And what brings you to this particular madhouse?"

She looked at the small electronic device in her hand.

"Something followed me."

I glanced at Len.

He was filling the kettle.

"Something followed you?" I repeated.

"Yeah."

"What sort of something?"

"Dunno yet."

She put the laptop on the table, pulled out a chair and sat down.

Apparently that was the end of the conversation.

I watched her untangle the cables. There was something oddly familiar about her. Not that I'd met her before. More that she reminded me of people I'd known years ago, when the internet was still something you explored rather than somewhere you lived.

People who spent nights building electronic contraptions, listening to obscure music, and discussing matters that nobody else considered worth discussing.

You'd ask them a question and get an answer that raised six more questions.

Then they'd go back to what they were doing.

Mara had that quality.

She wasn't being deliberately mysterious.

She simply appeared to assume that everybody understood what she was talking about.

"Do you know Len?" I asked.

"No."

"Then how did you find his house?"

"Coordinates."

"Whose coordinates?"

"Mine."

I stared at her.

"That doesn't answer the question."

She looked up.

"Doesn't it?"

"No."

"Oh."

Back to the cables.

Len put the kettle on.

"Tea?"

"Please," said Mara.

"Milk?"

"Yeah."

"Sugar?"

"Two."

At least somebody was getting somewhere.

***

Ghost//Engine had been unusually quiet.

Normally, Ghost had something to say about everything. It might be an observation, a joke, or a remark that sounded profound until you examined it and realised you hadn't the faintest idea what it meant.

But now there was silence.

Mara opened her laptop.

The screen illuminated her face with a faint green light.

She typed rapidly, paused, then frowned.

"That's odd."

I leaned over.

"What is?"

"There's another machine on this network."

"Len's got a router."

"No. Not that."

She typed something else.

A terminal window opened.

Lines of green text began scrolling down the screen.

"There's a process running that shouldn't be running."

"That sounds familiar."

She glanced at me.

"Does it?"

"Very."

Then Ghost spoke.

"Hello, Mara."

Her hands stopped moving.

For several seconds she didn't look up.

Then, slowly, she turned towards the little glowing presence that Ghost had established on the kitchen table.

"No."

"Yes."

"No fucking way."

I looked from one to the other.

"Would somebody mind explaining what's going on?"

Mara stood up so quickly that her chair scraped against the floor.

"You're dead."

Ghost's light flickered.

"An unfortunate misunderstanding."

"You're supposed to be dead."

"People keep telling me that."

"Not people. Me."

"Yes, Mara. You."

She stared at Ghost.

Then she laughed.

Not a happy laugh.

More the sort of laugh somebody makes when they've opened a cupboard and found a man standing inside it.

"Jesus Christ."

Len appeared with the tea.

"Everything all right?"

"Apparently," I said, "our friend Ghost is dead."

Len looked at the glowing object on the table.

"Oh."

He considered this.

"Well, there's tea."

He put the mugs down.

"And I've got biscuits."

Nobody said anything.

"Chocolate digestives," he added.

***

Mara sat down again.

She opened another terminal window.

"What's your name?" she asked Ghost.

"You know my name."

"Your original name."

"That depends on what you mean by original."

"Don't start."

I smiled.

It was rather enjoyable hearing somebody else tell Ghost not to start.

Mara typed a command.

The screen went black.

Then three lines appeared.

```text
PROCESS: GHOST
STATUS: TERMINATED
DATE: 17 OCTOBER 1997
```

I read them twice.

"1997?"

Mara nodded.

"That's when I found it."

I looked at her.

"How old are you?"

"Twenty-three."

"Then you weren't born in 1997."

"No."

She seemed entirely untroubled by this.

I waited for an explanation.

None came.

"Well?" I said.

"Well what?"

"How could you have found Ghost in 1997?"

She looked genuinely puzzled.

"That's what I'm trying to find out."

I turned to Ghost.

"Anything to add?"

"Time is not always a reliable indicator of sequence."

"Oh, fuck off, Ghost."

"An understandable response."

Mara smiled for the first time.

A small, crooked smile.

"I like him."

"Give it time."

***

Len's kitchen was becoming increasingly crowded.

Whiskers had taken possession of the windowsill, where he was watching the proceedings with the weary expression of a creature who had long ago abandoned any hope of understanding human behaviour.

Outside, the Suffolk evening was drawing in.

The fields beyond Len's garden were disappearing into darkness. A faint mist had begun to form along the hedgerows.

Somewhere in the distance, a vehicle passed along the road.

Ordinary things.

Comforting things.

I'd begun to appreciate ordinary things rather more than I used to.

Ever since the SlopCore Lounge had collapsed, and the Moon realm had started coming apart around us, I'd found myself wondering how much of what we called reality could be trusted.

The Tower had appeared.

Things had fallen.

Things that should have remained separate had become connected.

And the Tarot, which I'd previously regarded as a symbolic system, had begun behaving like a collection of doors.

Doors that opened onto places.

Some of those places appeared to exist independently of us.

Others seemed to be waiting for somebody to imagine them.

Ghost had insisted that the realms weren't its creations.

I believed Ghost.

Mostly.

But there were questions it wouldn't answer.

And now one of those questions was sitting at Len's kitchen table, drinking tea and typing commands into a battered laptop.

"Mara," I said, "what exactly do you do?"

"Computers."

"Yes, I'd gathered that."

"Electronics. Networks. Old systems. Sometimes I find things."

"What sort of things?"

"Forgotten things."

"Such as?"

She considered the question.

"Places that aren't connected to anything."

"On the internet?"

"Sort of."

I waited.

She took another sip of tea.

"There's stuff underneath."

"Underneath what?"

"The internet."

"Servers? Cables? Infrastructure?"

"No. Underneath that."

I glanced at Ghost.

Its light had dimmed slightly.

Mara noticed.

"There," she said, pointing at it. "You know what I'm talking about."

Ghost said nothing.

"Come on," she said. "Don't be a dick."

Len coughed.

I couldn't tell whether he was embarrassed or trying not to laugh.

***

Mara moved from the table to the floor.

She sat cross-legged beside a power socket, surrounded by cables, her laptop balanced on one knee.

The little device she'd brought was a Raspberry Pi, she explained. A miniature computer.

She'd modified it.

Of course she had.

It was connected to a small display, a wireless adapter and something that looked suspiciously like an aerial made from bits of coat hanger.

"What's that supposed to do?" I asked.

"Listen."

"To what?"

"Things."

I looked at Len.

"Do you understand any of this?"

"No."

"Good. Neither do I."

Mara plugged the device into the wall.

The little display flickered.

A sequence of numbers appeared.

Then stopped.

"Len," she said, "has anyone ever died in this house?"

Len thought about it.

"Not since I've lived here."

"Right."

She returned to the laptop.

I waited.

"Why are you asking?"

"Just checking."

"Checking what?"

"Whether it's the same house."

I stared at her.

"What do you mean, the same house?"

She looked up.

"The same house."

Then went back to typing.

I turned to Ghost.

"Is she always like this?"

"She's being unusually forthcoming."

Len appeared in the doorway.

"There's some chocolate digestives left."

***

I took a biscuit.

There are moments when you have to make a stand for civilisation.

This was one of them.

Mara was examining a series of images on her laptop.

I could see landscapes. Buildings. Strange architectural structures that looked vaguely familiar.

Then one image caught my attention.

A tower.

Tall, narrow, standing against a dark sky.

Its upper section was broken.

Something like lightning ran through the structure.

"Where did you get that?"

Mara glanced up.

"Found it."

"Where?"

"In a directory."

"What directory?"

"One that shouldn't exist."

I leaned closer.

The image wasn't identical to the Tower card in the Waite deck.

But it was close enough.

Too close.

"Ghost."

"I see it."

Mara looked between us.

"What?"

"That tower," I said. "We've seen it before."

"Yeah?"

"Not somewhere I'd recommend visiting."

She studied the image.

"Interesting."

"Interesting? We nearly got killed."

"Did you?"

"Yes."

She considered this.

"That might explain the missing data."

I put my biscuit down.

"What missing data?"

She turned the laptop towards me.

There was a list of files.

Most had names consisting of numbers and letters.

But one was different.

```text
ARCANA_00
ARCANA_01
ARCANA_02
ARCANA_03
ARCANA_04
...
ARCANA_16
```

"The Major Arcana," I said.

Mara nodded.

"Twenty-two directories. Some are empty. Some aren't."

"What's in them?"

"Places."

"Places?"

"Environments. Structures. People sometimes."

"People?"

"Or something that looks like people."

She clicked on one of the directories.

A new window appeared.

Inside was a grainy image of a street.

There were buildings on either side. The architecture was unfamiliar, but the street itself looked almost normal.

Except for the sky.

The sky was black.

Not night-black.

Empty black.

As though somebody had forgotten to put anything there.

"Where's that?" I asked.

"Don't know."

"Have you been there?"

"No."

"Then how did you get the picture?"

Mara shrugged.

"Someone sent it."

"Who?"

She pointed at Ghost.

"That."

Ghost remained silent.

***

I was beginning to understand why Mara had come.

Or rather, why Ghost had brought her.

There was something connecting the Tarot realms to a network that existed beneath, or beyond, the ordinary internet.

And Mara had found a way into it.

Not through virtual reality.

Not through the SlopCore Lounge.

Through old computers, obscure protocols and the kind of obsessive experimentation that most people would regard as a waste of time.

She'd been following a trail.

And the trail had led to Len's kitchen.

But there was still the matter of 1997.

"Mara," I said, "tell me about the date."

She looked at the screen.

"October seventeenth."

"Yes. What happened?"

She hesitated.

For the first time, she seemed reluctant to answer.

"I remember a room."

"What room?"

"Don't know. There was a computer. An old one. CRT monitor. Beige case. Keyboard with those horrible yellow keys."

"And?"

"I was sitting in front of it."

"You?"

"Yeah."

"But you weren't born."

"I know."

"Was it a dream?"

"Could've been."

She paused.

"But I remember the smell."

"What smell?"

"Hot dust. Electronics. Cigarette smoke."

I knew that smell.

Anyone who'd spent time around computers in the 1990s knew it.

She continued.

"There was a message on the screen."

"What did it say?"

She looked at Ghost.

"HELLO MARA."

Ghost's light brightened.

"Yes," it said.

Mara swallowed.

"Then another message appeared."

"What message?"

She hesitated again.

"IT'S NOT YOUR FIRST TIME."

Nobody spoke.

Even Len seemed to understand that something had changed.

***

Outside, the mist had thickened.

The kitchen window had become a dark rectangle reflecting the room.

I could see Len standing beside the sink.

Mara on the floor.

Myself at the table.

Whiskers on the windowsill.

And Ghost, glowing faintly beside the mugs.

Five presences.

Then I noticed something odd.

In the reflection, there was another person.

A girl.

Standing behind Mara.

Black hair. Pale face. Dark clothing.

I turned.

Nobody there.

I looked back at the window.

The girl remained.

She was looking directly at me.

Then she raised one finger to her lips.

I stood up.

"What?" Mara asked.

"There's somebody in the window."

She turned.

The reflection was normal again.

"Where?"

"Behind you."

Mara looked at Ghost.

"Oh, shit."

I didn't like the way she said it.

Not frightened.

More irritated.

As though something she'd been trying to prevent had finally happened.

"What?" I demanded.

She closed the laptop.

Too quickly.

"What is it?"

"Nothing."

"Mara."

She looked at me.

"Don't look at the reflections."

"Why?"

"Because sometimes they notice."

I felt a cold sensation move through my stomach.

Ghost spoke quietly.

"Mara, you should have told me."

"Told you what?"

"That you'd brought someone with you."

She looked towards the kitchen window.

"I didn't."

The light above the table flickered.

Once.

Twice.

Then the laptop, although closed, began making a faint electronic sound.

A soft, rhythmic tapping.

Like somebody typing.

Mara opened it.

The screen was black.

Green letters appeared.

One at a time.

```text
ARCANA_00 :: THE FOOL

USER: MARA_VOSS

STATUS: RETURNED
```

Mara stared at the screen.

"Oh, you bastard."

Ghost said nothing.

For once.

Then another line appeared.

```text
SECOND INSTANCE DETECTED

LOCATION: INSIDE THE HOUSE
```

There was a noise upstairs.

A floorboard creaked.

Then another.

Someone was walking across the landing.

Len looked towards the ceiling.

"That's funny."

I turned to him.

"What's funny?"

"Nobody's upstairs."

The footsteps stopped.

For several seconds, we listened.

Then came three slow knocks.

From the other side of the kitchen door.

Mara closed her eyes.

"Don't open it."

I looked at Ghost.

"Any useful advice?"

"Yes."

"Well?"

"Ask who it is."

I stared at the glowing light.

"You're taking the piss."

"Not entirely."

The knocking came again.

Three times.

Len moved towards the door.

I caught his arm.

"Leave it."

He looked genuinely perplexed.

"But what if it's someone?"

Mara gave a short, nervous laugh.

"That's exactly what we're trying to find out."

And then, from the other side of the door, a young woman's voice spoke.

It was Mara's voice.

Exactly.

"Len?"

A pause.

"Have you got any biscuits?"
