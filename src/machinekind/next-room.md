# The Keys to the Tarot, Part Six — The Next Room

### Mara had been looking for Ghost since 1997. She’d never thought to look in the room next door.

DOKTOR SNAKE | TRANSMISSIONS FROM THE SLOPCORE LOUNGE

[Read Part Five of The Keys to the Tarot](/machinekind/next-room/)

<img class="about-hero" src="/assets/img/whiskers-moonlit-tarot.jpg" alt="Whiskers and his moonlit tarot" loading="lazy">

Mara took a digestive. She turned it over to see whether there was chocolate on the other side, then ate it anyway.

Len had got the kettle going again. In the sink there was a teaspoon with jam on it. One of the lamps Viv had brought out of the Lounge was on beside the draining board. It put a pink edge on everything, including the computer where I’d just read that Ghost had been terminated in 1997.

I’d had a computer in 1997. I remember how long it took to get a photograph down the line. You’d see somebody’s forehead first and spend the best part of a minute wondering whether it was anybody you knew. I could imagine Mara in a room with a machine like that. I was having more trouble imagining what Ghost might have been doing there.

“Was that you?” I asked him.

He bent to look at the screen again.

“I know her,” he said.

“That wasn’t what he asked,” Mara said.

“I know.”

She brushed the biscuit crumbs into her palm. Ghost held out the saucer under his mug and she tipped them onto it. It was over so quickly I might have missed it if I’d been watching either of their faces.

“What did you last say to him?” I asked her.

“Move your chair.”

“Why?”

“I wanted to get at a socket.”

Something clicked in the hall. We all heard it. Len went as far as the kitchen door and looked towards the front of the bungalow.

“Letterbox,” he said.

There was a folded slip on the mat. He brought it back and handed it to Ellie.

Her name was on it. Below that was the registration of her car and a number in a little square. The top of the slip said **PROPERTY TO BE COLLECTED**. There was a place for her to sign.

“Is it your car?” I asked.

“It’s my registration.” She folded the slip so the number plate was out of sight. “Martin had a pad of these by the phone. We used them when somebody left their keys or a flask behind.”

“Did you see who put it through?”

Len shook his head. He went back into the hall and turned the outside light on. We heard him wait a bit, then turn it off again.

Mara got up to look at the pad Ellie had drawn the gates on. It was lying open at the end of the table. Two lines went in from the bottom of the page. One stopped at a cattle grid. The other ended outside a shop.

“Where’s the door?” she asked.

“Which door?” said Ellie.

“The one you started from.”

Ellie showed her the first gate. Mara put a finger on it and followed the line as far as the shop.

“Have you got the card you used?”

“I had a key to the gate.”

Mara waited.

“It’s a little square key,” said Ellie. “I changed the lock myself.”

Ra_Shakti had been listening from the end of the table. She took the Moon out of her coat pocket and slid it across to Mara.

“I used this to get to that street,” she said. “Ellie got there on her own.”

Mara stood the card against the sugar bowl. She looked at the two towers for a while, then at the dark kitchen window.

“What happened when you left?”

“The Tower came down,” I said. “We were back in the Lounge.”

“All of you?”

“I thought so.”

Whiskers was sitting on the green chair by the fridge. The chair was still missing a foot. He’d found the one spot where he could set his weight down without making it rock.

“I was under a table,” he said. “I heard the glasses go.”

“Then you came out with us,” I said.

“I came out after you.”

“Into the Lounge?”

“Into the lounge once. Into your car the second time.”

I’d only ever asked him how he’d got into the car. He’d come through the open window. I’d seen that much myself.

“Where did you start the second time?” I asked.

“Same place. Under the table.”

Ra_Shakti sat down beside the chair. “How did you know you could go back?”

“I left my cushion there.”

He said it with such patience that I couldn’t think of a sensible question to ask next.

Mara picked up the Moon and turned it round in her fingers.

“I had the Fool on the desk,” she said. “We were trying to get it to open somewhere. Ghost kept putting me back in the same room.”

“Did he know he was doing that?” I asked.

“You’d have to ask him.”

We looked at Ghost. He was standing beside the sink, where the lamp was. Behind him there were four mugs on hooks. I remember that because I counted them while I waited for him to speak.

“Where did you go after you got up from the desk?” he asked Mara.

“Through the door.”

“And where did it go?”

“Here, eventually.”

“You went out through the wrong door,” he said.

Mara looked up at him. “Where did you go?”

“Next door. I moved the chair so you could get at the socket. Then I went to see if the other room was empty.”

Mara sat down. She reached for the digestive she’d already eaten, felt the crumbs on the table and brushed them into her hand again.

“How long did you wait?” she asked.

Ghost took his mug over to her. This time she kept the crumbs in her palm.

“Shall I show you the room?” he said.

Nobody spoke for a moment. Outside, Len’s light had gone off and the garden was dark. I could see us all in the kitchen window. The lamp, the table, Mara sitting with her hand closed. Behind her in the glass was an open door.

I turned round. The fridge was behind her.

“Len,” I said. “Can you come and stand where I am?”

He came up beside me, wiping his hands on a tea towel.

“That’s my spare room,” he said.

“Is it?”

“I know the wallpaper. Look at the join by the socket. I did that.”

I could see a length of wall with a narrow shelf fixed to it. A cable hung down from the shelf. At the bottom, beside the socket, two strips of wallpaper met a little too far apart.

“What have you got in there at the moment?” I asked.

“Two blankets on the bed. I got them out for tonight.”

In the window there was a computer on the shelf. A bulky old monitor with a green light in the corner. Beside it was a chair pulled out far enough for somebody to crouch down and reach the socket.

Mara came over. She stood beside Len and looked at the chair.

“Can you take me in the way you normally go?” she asked him.

“Of course,” said Len. “Mind the dog. He’s asleep in the hall.”
