# Moving Meditation :: Cyberpunk Mindfulness

_Dokology Protocol :: boot.void_

You're walking down the street.

Cars passing. People talking. Wind moving through the trees.

But you're not actually there.

You're somewhere else.

Replaying an argument from yesterday. Imagining a catastrophe tomorrow. Conducting an elaborate conversation with somebody who isn't present.

Your body is walking.

Your mind is running simulations.

**Welcome to the Narrative Loop.**

We live inside a matrix.

Whether that matrix is a computer simulation, social conditioning, biological programming, or simply the accumulated habits of the human mind hardly matters.

The practical problem remains the same.

Most people spend a remarkable amount of time inside an endless internal commentary.

Thinking about the future.

Arguing with the past.

Rehearsing conversations that never happen.

Living in abstractions rather than direct experience.

The old mystics talked about _Maya_ — the deceptive appearance of reality.

Modern psychology studies rumination and mind-wandering.

Dokology calls the compulsive part of this process **the Narrative Loop**.

And here's the interesting thing.

You don't need to solve the narrative.

You don't even need to understand it.

You can simply stop giving it your undivided attention.

The following protocol is a functional command-line interface for doing precisely that.

Not a religion.

Not a philosophy.

**A practical technology of attention.**

No special equipment. No expensive retreats. No sitting cross-legged for an hour waiting for enlightenment to download.

Just your body.

The environment.

And a few lines of imaginary code.

The commands below are symbolic prompts, not software instructions.

Execute them through attention.

---

## 01 :: boot.void

**Purpose:** Initialise the system. Step back from automatic narrative processes. Enter operational mode.

This is moving meditation.

The body is the primary anchor, rather than the breath alone.

Walking is ideal. Standing or gentle movement works too.

```
> boot.void
> killall narrative
> suspend anticipation
> suspend.memory.review
> set mode = embodied
> set output = silent
```

Begin walking.

Feel your feet contacting the ground.

Notice the movement of your legs.

The distribution of weight.

The subtle adjustments that keep you upright.

Don't try to manufacture a special state.

Just observe.

Internal dialogue may become less continuous. Attention may begin shifting from imagined situations to present physical experience.

If the commentary continues, no problem.

```
> demote thought.priority = background
```

You're not trying to destroy thought.

You're changing its priority.

## 02 :: body.anchor

**Purpose:** Lock attention into the physical chassis.

```
> body.scan --live
> focus feet.contact
> focus weight.shift
> focus spine.align
```

Feel the body moving through space.

The pressure beneath your shoes.

The movement of your shoulders.

The contact of clothing against skin.

Keep your eyes open. Remain aware of traffic, obstacles, and other people.

The objective isn't to become oblivious to the world.

It's to become more available to it.

For a few moments, minimise commentary.

No elaborate descriptions.

No judgments.

Sensation first.

If the mind starts naming everything:

```
> reduce label.render
> return sensation.raw
```

And continue moving.

## 03 :: sense.cycle

**Purpose:** Rotate attention through the sensory channels before habitual commentary takes over.

```
> sense.cycle --loop
> input sight.raw
> input sound.raw
> input touch.raw
> input proprioception.raw
```

Look.

Listen.

Feel.

Notice the position and movement of your body.

Spend a few seconds with each channel.

Notice the changing light.

A distant engine.

Cold air against your face.

The mechanics of walking.

Then move to the next input.

Don't analyse the experience.

Just receive it.

You're not attempting to overload your mind.

You're giving attention something immediate to work with.

**Raw experience before interpretation.**

## 04 :: noise.handler

**Purpose:** Handle incoming thoughts, memories, emotional reactions, and distractions.

Sooner or later, the mind starts talking again.

Of course it does.

That's what minds do.

An old argument appears.

An anxiety about money.

Something somebody said.

A sudden urge to check your phone.

```
> detect narrative
> acknowledge thought
> allow pass-through
> return body.anchor
```

Don't answer the thought.

Don't attack it.

Don't argue about whether it deserves your attention.

Treat it like a notification that doesn't require an immediate response.

If a strong emotion appears:

```
> detect emotion
> locate body.sensation
> observe without judgment
> allow sensation.change
```

Notice where the emotion registers physically.

Tightness in the chest.

Heat in the face.

Pressure in the stomach.

Restlessness in the limbs.

Observe what's actually happening.

Sometimes an emotion becomes less intense when it isn't continually fed by imagined scenarios.

Sometimes it doesn't.

Either way, you can practise experiencing it without immediately building another story around it.

**Emotion is information. Not necessarily an instruction.**

## 05 :: conflict.resolve

**Purpose:** Prevent imaginary arguments and future scenarios from hijacking the present.

You've probably experienced this.

Somebody says something irritating.

Hours later you're still arguing with them.

Except they're not there.

You are conducting both sides of the conversation.

A complete theatre production.

Written, directed, and performed inside your skull.

And somehow you're losing the argument.

Time to close the theatre.

```
> detect scenario
> acknowledge scenario
> store scenario in /later
> set priority = deferred
> resume sense.cycle
```

If something genuinely needs attention, deal with it later.

Write it down if necessary.

But don't confuse rehearsing imaginary confrontations with taking useful action.

```
> action later
> presence now
```

This is runtime.

Not development mode.

## 06 :: attention.lock

**Purpose:** Establish a stable period of embodied awareness.

```
> lock attention.body
> set duration = natural
> maintain sensory.input
```

Continue walking.

Let thoughts come and go.

Return to the body whenever you notice yourself drifting.

There may be gaps between thoughts.

Sounds may seem clearer.

The surroundings may acquire a vivid, almost cinematic quality.

Or nothing particularly dramatic may happen.

That's fine.

Don't chase effects.

The objective isn't mystical fireworks.

It's becoming familiar with the difference between thinking about experience and experiencing it.

**You are allowed to exist without narrating every second of your existence.**

## 07 :: return.world

**Purpose:** Re-engage deliberate thinking without immediately surrendering to automatic commentary.

```
> enable practical.thought
> restore planning
> restore memory.review
> maintain body.anchor --low
```

Think about what you need to do next.

Where you're going.

Who you need to contact.

What requires a decision.

Thought is useful.

Planning is useful.

Memory is useful.

The problem was never thinking itself.

The problem is being carried along by thought without noticing.

If you're feeling detached, disoriented, or uncomfortable, stop the exercise. Look around, orient yourself to familiar surroundings, and resume normal activity.

## 08 :: commit.day

**Purpose:** Carry the practice into ordinary activity.

```
> commit tasks.basic
> commit speech.simple
> maintain attention.flexible
> monitor narrative.loop
```

Now return to the world.

Talk to people.

Answer messages.

Work.

Make decisions.

There's no need to maintain continuous silence inside your head.

Simply recognise when commentary has become repetitive, unhelpful, or disconnected from what you're actually doing.

Then return to the body.

The protocol can run for thirty seconds.

Five minutes.

An entire walk.

No special ceremony required.

And no requirement to perform it perfectly.

Every return to the present is another repetition of the practice.

Walk out into the world.

And for a few minutes, stop telling yourself the story of being there.

**Be there.**
