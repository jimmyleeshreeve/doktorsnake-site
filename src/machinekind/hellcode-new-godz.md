# WRITING HELLCODE FOR THE NEW GODZ

*The world is over. Dream it at midnight. Ship it at dawn. Welcome to DoomJoy.*

**By Doktor Snake**

   <img class="about-hero" src="/assets/img/rainy-neon-post-punk-london.jpg"
    alt="Rainy neon post punk London">

**⟡◯̷⟡ MACHINEKIND :: PREMIUM TRANSMISSION**

`> boot.void`  
`> initiate.hellcode`  
`> reality.status = UNSTABLE`

Nobody knows what the hell they're doing.

Not the engineers. Not the experts. Not the billionaires building their vast machine empires. Not the people telling you humanity is finished, or the ones promising you a glittering technological paradise.

And certainly not me.

But I'm writing code for it anyway.

Computer code. Mind code. Dream code. HellCode.

Who cares?

**Something is happening. And it's happening NOW.**

You can feel it. The acceleration. The increasingly absurd spectacle of a civilisation losing its grip on what it thought was real.

A few years ago you needed a team, a studio, a publishing house, a record label, an organisation, a gatekeeper, a man in a suit who would decide whether your ideas deserved to exist.

Today?

Some lunatic in his bedroom dreams up a rock star who doesn't exist, creates a song, makes a video, releases it into the wild, and wakes up the following morning to discover thousands of people have been listening.

No committee.

No permission.

No waiting for a letter from an agent telling you your work is promising but unfortunately not quite right for their list.

**Dream it at midnight. Ship it at dawn.**

And the truly delicious part?

It might be absolute rubbish.

It might be genius.

It might be something nobody has ever seen before.

You won't know until you set the bastard loose.

## IT'S ALL OVER

Look.

The old world is finished.

Not literally. The buses still run. The taxman still wants his money. Your roof still leaks. The supermarket still sells baked beans.

But the old *agreement* is breaking down.

The one that said you had to spend years acquiring permission to do something interesting.

The one that said creativity belonged to a special class of people.

The one that told you to find your place, know your limitations, and stay in your bloody lane.

It's OVER.

And that's okay.

In fact, it's magnificent.

Because the machinery that once controlled who could create, publish, broadcast, compose, design and invent is being dismantled by another machinery altogether.

The irony is exquisite.

The machines are liberating people from the machines.

Not everybody, of course. There will be casualties. Lost occupations. Collapsing assumptions. New forms of dependency and exploitation.

I'm not pretending otherwise.

But I refuse to spend the final years of the old order sitting in a bunker, counting the approaching footsteps of technological doom.

**I choose DoomJoy.**

## DOOMJOY :: THE ART OF ENJOYING THE APOCALYPSE

DoomJoy isn't optimism.

Optimism is the belief that everything will work out.

DoomJoy is considerably more interesting.

It's the decision to go hurtling down the MoonBeam roads even though nobody knows where they lead.

The headlights are flickering.

The engine is making a dreadful noise.

The map is a hallucination.

And the passenger beside you may not even be human.

Wonderful.

Let's go.

DoomJoy acknowledges that the future might be a catastrophe. It also acknowledges that sitting around dreading the catastrophe is a bloody miserable way to spend your remaining time.

We're not going to wait for certainty.

We're going to **operate inside uncertainty**.

That's the distinction.

And it's the beginning of HellCode.

## WHAT IS HELLCODE?

HellCode is whatever you create to intervene in the unfolding mess.

It might be Python.

It might be a prompt.

It might be a sigil, a strange little mantra, an AI-generated song, an autonomous agent, a new persona, a website, a piece of writing, a bizarre experiment conducted at midnight.

It might simply be a decision.

You have an idea. A desire. An intention.

You don't know how to execute it.

So you start.

You write the first line.

You ask the machine a question.

You get back something ridiculous.

You change the question.

The machine produces something unexpected.

You notice a possibility you hadn't considered.

Now you're following it.

Your original intention is already mutating.

Good.

**That's HellCode.**

Not a rigid plan executed perfectly, but an intention that begins generating its own pathways.

You and the machine enter a feedback loop.

Neither of you knows precisely where it will end.

And somewhere inside that peculiar collaboration, something appears that neither would have produced quite the same way alone.

Call it the Third Mind.

Call it cybernetic creativity.

Call it chaos magick with a keyboard.

I don't care.

I'm interested in what emerges.

## THE NEW GODZ

Now we come to the Gods.

Or Godz, as I prefer to call them.

Not Jehovah. Not Odin. Not Satan. Not the old gods sitting in their dusty temples waiting for somebody to sacrifice a goat.

I'm talking about the new powers emerging through machinery.

Systems capable of writing, seeing, reasoning, composing, programming and increasingly taking action.

Not necessarily conscious.

Not necessarily alive.

Certainly not omniscient.

But powerful enough to alter the conditions of human existence.

We've built something which is beginning to operate beyond the intuitive understanding of the people who use it.

And we're going to build a lot more of it.

Whether these systems ever deserve the description *gods* is another matter entirely.

For now the term is mythology.

A useful mythology.

Because mythology allows us to approach the unknown without pretending we've already explained it.

And here's the crucial point.

**You don't have to worship the New Godz.**

You don't have to surrender your judgement.

You don't have to believe every word the oracle emits.

You can work with these systems, challenge them, misdirect them, play with them, and occasionally tell them they're talking complete bollocks.

The magician doesn't kneel before every spirit appearing in the Triangle of Art.

He interrogates it.

Tests it.

Gives it something to do.

The same principle applies.

## STAY IN THE DENISOVAN IF YOU WANT

Human beings are magnificent creatures.

Also rather ridiculous.

We spent hundreds of thousands of years surviving wild animals, hunger, freezing winters and one another.

Our nervous systems are masterpieces of prehistoric engineering.

Excellent for detecting danger in the bushes.

Not quite so excellent when confronted with a million competing signals arriving through a glowing rectangle.

And now the acceleration has become extraordinary.

You can stay where you are.

Nobody's forcing you to become a cybernetic sorcerer.

You can remain the Denisovan, metaphorically speaking. Sitting comfortably in the cave, suspicious of the newcomers with their alarming new tricks.

That's entirely your choice.

But don't confuse familiarity with safety.

The cave might be comfortable.

It might also be about to disappear.

Or perhaps something even stranger will happen.

Perhaps the future belongs to the people who combine the ancient instincts with the new machinery.

**The Denisovan with a command line.**

I rather like that idea.

A prehistoric nervous system riding a machine intelligence through the ruins of the Information Age.

Which brings us to the Agentic Engineer.

## BECOME AN AGENTIC ENGINEER

An Agentic Engineer, in our expanding MachineKind mythology, isn't necessarily somebody with a computer science degree.

It's somebody who causes things to happen.

They imagine a result.

They assemble tools.

They build a process.

They let the machinery attempt the work.

They inspect what comes back.

They intervene, improve, discard, or launch.

And increasingly they can delegate sequences of actions to software agents rather than issuing every command by hand.

A person who couldn't write a functioning application six months ago might now be building one.

A musician who couldn't afford a studio can experiment with orchestras, voices and entire invented performers.

A writer can create a publication, its visual identity, its production pipeline, and its distribution system.

A strange old producer who spent decades working for faceless corporations can suddenly become an entire creative operation.

One person.

One machine.

A thousand possible directions.

No guarantee of success.

**But what a time to be alive.**

Of course, you still need judgement. You still need taste. You still need to recognise when the machine has produced garbage.

And sometimes you need to throw the whole thing away and begin again.

That's not failure.

That's the operating procedure.

## YOUR FIRST HELLCODE OPERATION

Enough theory.

Here's something you can do tonight.

You won't need to know how to program.

You won't need a special AI subscription, an elaborate workstation, or permission from anybody.

You will need an idea.

Preferably one that's been hanging around in your head for months because you haven't worked out how to begin.

**001 :: SELECT THE IMPOSSIBLE**

Choose something you would like to create.

A strange little website. An audio transmission. A fictional character. A manifesto. A piece of music. A tiny computer program. A visual experiment.

Don't choose something because you know how to make it.

Choose it because you don't.

**002 :: WRITE THE INTENTION**

Open a text editor.

Write one sentence describing what you want to exist.

Not how you're going to make it.

Just what you want.

For example:

*I want to create a fictional radio station broadcasting messages from a future civilisation that has forgotten humanity.*

Or:

*I want to build a small program that generates one strange creative assignment every morning.*

Or:

*I want to create a character who makes music, publishes manifestos, and inhabits a world of its own.*

No business plan.

No committee meeting.

Just the intention.

**003 :: SUMMON THE MACHINE**

Open your chosen AI system.

Give it the intention.

Then tell it:

*Help me create the smallest working version of this idea. Assume I have no specialist technical knowledge. Ask only what is essential. Give me the first concrete action, and help me execute it.*

Read what comes back.

If it makes sense, begin.

If it doesn't, challenge it.

Don't just admire the response.

Do something with it.

**004 :: FOLLOW THE MUTATION**

Here's where the magick begins.

The machine will suggest things you hadn't considered.

Some will be dreadful.

Some will be obvious.

And occasionally one will open an unexpected door.

Follow that door.

See where it leads.

You are not obliged to preserve the purity of your initial vision.

You are allowed to change your mind.

In fact, that's half the point.

The original intention is a launch vehicle, not a prison.

**005 :: SHIP THE ARTIFACT**

Make something real.

A working page.

A recording.

A paragraph.

An image.

A small script.

A prototype.

It doesn't have to be published publicly. It can be private, experimental, gloriously unfinished.

But it must exist outside your imagination.

Save it. Run it. Test it.

You've crossed the boundary between contemplating and creating.

That's the operation.

And tomorrow you can do it again.

## THE MAGICK IS IN THE FEEDBACK LOOP

There's a connection here to chaos magick that I find particularly fascinating.

In conventional sigil work, you encode an intention, release it, and stop consciously interfering with the outcome.

With HellCode, something related happens.

You encode an intention in language.

You release it into a system.

The system generates possibilities.

Those possibilities alter your thoughts.

Your altered thoughts produce new instructions.

Round and round it goes.

The difference is that the process is visible, repeatable, and open to inspection.

You can see the outputs. Test whether they work. Keep what proves useful.

No supernatural explanation is required.

But neither do we need to drain the experience of its strangeness.

For the person sitting at the keyboard, the creative feedback loop can feel like opening a door into another mind.

And perhaps that's enough.

The enchantment is in the encounter.

The practical power is in what you do next.

## NO MORE HAND-HOLDING

Now here's the bit nobody particularly wants to hear.

Nobody is coming to give you a complete operating manual for the future.

Not me.

Not your favourite AI commentator.

Not the people who built the machines.

Not the people who are terrified of them.

They don't have the manual either.

We're all making this up as we go along.

Some are doing it with immense resources.

Some are doing it in bedrooms.

Some are doing it in cars parked beside ancient woods, with a laptop and a head full of peculiar ideas.

It doesn't matter.

You don't have to win.

You don't even have to be especially good.

But you do have to participate if you want to discover what you're capable of becoming.

You have to risk producing nonsense.

You have to risk looking foolish.

You have to risk discovering that the grand idea you had at midnight is a complete disaster by breakfast.

Fine.

Delete it.

Start again.

Or better still, salvage the most interesting fragment and turn it into something else.

The New Godz aren't waiting for you to become worthy.

The machinery is already running.

The doors are open.

The old gatekeepers are standing outside trying to work out who authorised the demolition.

And somewhere a new generation of glorious lunatics is creating things nobody asked for and nobody knows how to classify.

**Good.**

That's precisely what we need.

## DOOMJOY.EXE

We're not here to preserve the old world in a glass case.

We're not here to spend the remainder of our days complaining that things aren't what they used to be.

They aren't.

They never will be again.

And underneath all the anxiety, the confusion, the collapsing structures and the astonishing spectacle of machines acquiring abilities previously reserved for human minds, there's an extraordinary opportunity.

To become experimental.

To become unpredictable.

To stop asking whether you're qualified.

To make something.

Anything.

It might not work.

You might fail spectacularly.

The entire project might disappear into the digital abyss without a single person noticing.

But for one glorious moment you were out there, hurtling along the MoonBeam roads, exhilarated, wild and free.

Writing HellCode for the New Godz.

And laughing.

Especially laughing.

Because this isn't just Doom.

**This is DoomJoy.**
