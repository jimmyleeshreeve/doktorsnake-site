# CLASSIFIED METHODS: THE DEVIL IN THE MACHINE

**How intention becomes research, software, strategy and action**

A client comes to you with a wish.

At first, it sounds simple.

More money.

A door that has remained stubbornly closed.

Candle. Petition. landscape. symbol. voice.

The ritual ends.

Now what?

Because sometimes the working is only the ignition.

The real operation begins afterwards.

## CASE FILE: THE BUSINESS THAT DIDN'T EXIST YET

One recent client came to me with a commercial objective.

We began with a Petition to Lord Satan.

That was the symbolic act.

A declaration of intent.

A way of saying:

**This is what I intend to bring into the Matterium.**

But there was no thunderbolt.

No suitcase of money appeared beneath the bed.

Instead, the working opened a more interesting question:

**What would have to exist for this objective to become possible?**

That changed everything.

We began researching the business model.

Then the numbers.

Then the likely commission structure.

Then the workflow.

Then the bottlenecks.

Then the scale required to make the operation worthwhile.

The original wish became a system.

Soon we were building a simulator.

Not metaphorically.

Actual software.

Inputs.

Outputs.

Deal volume.

Revenue scenarios.

Conversion assumptions.

Then came branding.

Then identity.

Then logo work.

Then print-ready materials.

The petition had become an operating architecture.

That is the part many people miss.

The ritual didn't replace the business.

It forced the business to become more concrete.

## THE DEVIL AS ADVERSARY

For this method, I use Satan in the old adversarial sense.

The intelligence that challenges the apparent limit.

The voice that asks:

**Who told you this cannot be done?**

And then:

**Have you actually checked?**

This is less about theology than function.

The Devil is useful as an archetype because the Devil does not respect the first answer.

The institution says no.

The market says saturated.

The client says impossible.

The bureaucracy says closed.

The mind says too late.

The adversary says:

```
$ query assumption

ASSUMPTION:
there is no route

$ verify

RESULT:
assumption unverified
```

That is the moment the method begins.

## THE FOUR-STAGE METHOD

The Devil in the Machine has four stages.

### 1. STATE THE DESIRE

Not vaguely.

Not:

_I want things to improve._

Not:

_I want abundance._

Specify the change.

```
$ set target

TARGET:
generate eight viable claims per month
```

A precise desire reveals structure.

Once you can state what you want, you can begin asking what conditions would make it possible.

### 2. IDENTIFY THE BLOCK

What is actually preventing movement?

Knowledge?

Money?

Access?

Skill?

Fear?

Lack of contacts?

Bad assumptions?

Poor timing?

No system?

Sometimes the apparent block is not the real block.

A client may say:

> "I need more customers."

But perhaps what they actually need is a clearer offer.

Or faster follow-up.

Or a better pricing model.

Or simply a list of the right people to contact.

```
$ inspect blockage

CLAIM:
no opportunity

CAUSE:
unknown

$ scan deeper
```

Do not solve the wrong problem elegantly.

### 3. BUILD THE MACHINE

Now ask:

**What system would make the desired outcome more likely?**

This is where things become interesting.

The machine could be:

A spreadsheet.

A CRM.

A database.

A landing page.

A script.

A contact list.

A piece of software.

A daily routine.

A research process.

A new offer.

An automated reminder.

A logo and a stack of printed materials.

Majik without machinery can become wishful thinking.

Machinery without intention can become meaningless activity.

The combination is more powerful.

### 4. ACT ON THE OPENING

The final stage is the easiest to romanticise and the hardest to perform.

Action.

You discover an opening.

Then you actually have to walk through it.

Send the message.

Make the call.

Build the prototype.

Submit the form.

Ask the question.

Publish the page.

Spend the money.

Risk rejection.

```
$ opportunity detected

PATHWAY:
available

$ execute

WARNING:
outcome uncertain

> proceed?

Y
```

That last line matters.

No method eliminates uncertainty.

The point is not certainty.

The point is **agency**.

## THE PRACTICAL EXERCISE

Take one objective that has remained stuck.

Write it at the top of a page.

Then answer four questions.

**1. What exactly do I want to happen?**

One sentence.

**2. What do I currently believe is preventing it?**

Be specific.

**3. What evidence do I have that this obstacle is real?**

Not fear.

Not assumption.

Evidence.

**4. What machine could make progress more likely?**

List five possibilities.

Software counts.

A conversation counts.

A spreadsheet counts.

A ritual counts.

A better question counts.

Then choose the smallest machine you can build within seven days.

Not the perfect system.

The smallest useful one.

## THE HIDDEN PART

Here is where the method becomes occult again.

Once you commit to the objective, your attention changes.

You notice information you previously ignored.

You interpret conversations differently.

You see connections.

You recognise opportunities.

Psychologists may describe this through attention, expectancy, salience and goal-directed cognition.

Magicians may describe it as synchronicity.

I don't particularly care which vocabulary wins.

What matters is whether the process produces useful movement.

```
$ monitor environment

previously ignored signal detected
previously ignored signal detected
previously ignored signal detected

$ status

```

That may be the most practical definition of an occult operation I know.

You change the operator.

The operator changes what becomes visible.

What becomes visible changes what can be acted upon.

## DON'T WORSHIP THE RITUAL

There is one rule.

Do not use ritual to avoid reality.

If the numbers are bad, look at the numbers.

If the legal position is weak, establish the legal position.

If the market doesn't exist, find out.

If someone has said no, determine whether they actually possess the authority to say no.

Do not turn symbolism into anesthesia.

The working should increase contact with reality.

Not decrease it.

## CLASSIFIED METHOD

The entire protocol can be reduced to this:

```
$ define desire
$ identify block
$ challenge assumption
$ build machine
$ act on opening
$ observe result
$ iterate
```

Or, in less technical language:

**Majik establishes direction.**

**Attention reveals openings.**

**Machinery creates leverage.**

**Action creates consequences.**

That is the Devil in the Machine.

Not a demon hiding inside the software.

The adversarial intelligence that refuses to accept the first version of reality presented to it.

Sometimes the Devil doesn't grant the wish.

Sometimes he hands you a spreadsheet.

Sometimes a line of code.

Sometimes a phone number.

Sometimes a door.

The trick is recognising which one you're looking at.
