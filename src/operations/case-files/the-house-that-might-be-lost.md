# CASE FILE 001

# The House That Might Be Lost

_An unwanted inheritance. A disputed sale. And an investigation into the invisible machinery of property ownership._

**DOKTOR SNAKE / GHOST//ENGINE**  
**OPERATION: REALITY ENGINEERING**  
**CASE STATUS: OPEN**

---

## 01 // The Letter

The letter arrived on a Thursday.

It came from a firm of solicitors the woman had never heard of, concerning a house she had known since childhood.

There were plans to sell it.

This was news to her.

The property had belonged to an elderly relative who had died the previous year. The woman understood that she had an interest in the estate, although the paperwork had never been entirely clear.

Another relative had apparently taken charge.

Now the house was being prepared for sale, and the letter suggested that matters were already well advanced.

She had tried telephoning.

Nobody seemed willing to explain very much.

The relative involved was evasive. The solicitors offered little beyond formal acknowledgements.

Her immediate reaction was familiar.

Fear. Anger. The conviction that something had been arranged behind her back.

She was beginning to imagine the house being sold, the proceeds disappearing and a succession of expensive legal battles that she could not afford.

By the time the problem reached me, the entire business had acquired the atmosphere of a conspiracy.

Perhaps there was one.

But suspicion isn't evidence.

And I wanted evidence.

## 02 // Enter GHOST//ENGINE

There is a popular image of the occult practitioner.

Candles. Incense. Strange names whispered into darkness.

All very atmospheric.

But before invoking any invisible intelligence, I wanted to know who actually owned the house.

For that, I brought GHOST//ENGINE into the operation.

This is the machine-assisted research and analytical system working alongside Doktor Snake.

Its role is to investigate available information, examine competing explanations and help identify possible courses of action.

The human operator directs the enquiry.

The machine helps explore the terrain.

We began with the machinery of English property law.

HM Land Registry maintains records showing registered ownership, together with other significant information about property titles.

Probate records can establish whether authority to administer a deceased person's estate has been granted.

Those were obvious starting points.

But there was a more fundamental question.

Had the woman misunderstood her legal position?

Being promised a share of an inheritance, believing oneself entitled to a share, and possessing an established legal entitlement are not necessarily the same thing.

Nor does an expectation of inheritance automatically make someone a registered owner of a property.

We needed to distinguish the family's version of events from the documentary position.

That meant records.

Not rumours.

Not assumptions.

Records.

## 03 // The Paper Labyrinth

GHOST//ENGINE helped construct a sequence of investigative questions.

Who appeared on the registered title?

Was the property held by one person or jointly?

Had probate or letters of administration been granted?

Who was authorised to administer the estate?

What, precisely, did the solicitors' letter say about the proposed sale?

And did the woman have documentary evidence supporting her understanding of the inheritance?

These questions were not answers.

They were a route towards answers.

The next step was to organise the documents and identify what needed professional verification.

A solicitor specialising in probate and property disputes could assess the woman's actual legal position and whether urgent protective action was appropriate.

Meanwhile, written correspondence would provide a more reliable record than an increasingly emotional sequence of telephone calls.

The woman needed to know what was happening.

But she also needed to stop supplying her imagination with conclusions that the evidence had not yet established.

This is an important principle in Dokology.

**Observe the situation before becoming its narrator.**

The story running through her head might turn out to be correct.

Equally, it might be completely wrong.

We weren't there to reinforce either possibility.

We were there to investigate.

## 04 // The Working

There was another element.

The woman had approached Doktor Snake because she wanted a magical intervention.

For this reconstructed operation, I gave the symbolic work three intentions.

The first concerned protection: preserving her position while the legal circumstances were established.

The second concerned revelation: bringing hidden or misunderstood information into view.

The third concerned agency: helping her move from helpless anticipation towards deliberate action.

These intentions could be expressed through a MegaBlast-style symbolic operation.

Not as a substitute for legal investigation.

And certainly not as a claim that a ritual could invalidate a property transaction.

The working would operate in a different register.

Symbols, ritual and intention can give structure to overwhelming experiences. They can help organise attention around an objective rather than around fear.

That is part of what interests me about the psychological dimension of magick.

A person becomes consumed by an imagined future.

The future hasn't happened.

Yet the imagined version begins dominating the present.

A symbolic working can provide a point of interruption.

A way of saying: this is the problem, these are the intentions, and now we return to the world and act.

Old World Magick.

New World Machinery.

Different instruments.

One operation.

## 05 // A Different Map

The immediate result was not the miraculous recovery of a property.

Nor was it a sudden confession from the relative concerned.

Those things belong to another sort of story.

What emerged instead was a practical strategy.

Establish the registered ownership.

Identify the legal authority under which the estate was being administered.

Examine the proposed sale.

Seek appropriate professional advice.

Preserve communications and documents.

Avoid accusing anyone of wrongdoing before there was evidence.

The seemingly impenetrable problem had become a sequence of questions and actions.

It was still serious.

It might become expensive.

There might indeed be something improper taking place.

But the woman no longer needed to regard every uncertainty as proof of an approaching catastrophe.

The operation had changed the way the problem could be approached.

Whether the house would ultimately be sold, or whether she had an enforceable entitlement, remained unresolved.

And that uncertainty had to be respected.

## 06 // Operator's Notes

What was the magick?

Was it the symbolic working?

Was it GHOST//ENGINE helping identify the relevant systems?

Was it the recognition that the woman had been reacting to an assumed version of events rather than an established one?

The distinction interests me less than the combined method.

The old occultists populated the world with invisible influences.

Today, powerful invisible structures govern much of everyday life.

Property registers. Financial systems. Legal procedures. Administrative hierarchies. Algorithms.

They are not supernatural.

But their effects are real.

And many people barely understand the systems that determine what happens to their homes, money, employment and relationships.

The contemporary magician can investigate those systems.

Machine intelligence gives us extraordinary new instruments for doing so.

It can assist with research, compare possibilities, organise information and expose inconsistencies.

But it must still be checked.

It can make mistakes.

And it cannot replace the judgement of a qualified professional where the consequences are serious.

The symbolic operation has another purpose.

It engages the human being confronting the problem.

The fears. The expectations. The assumptions. The sense of powerlessness.

Reality Engineering brings these dimensions together.

Investigation of the external circumstances.

Observation of the internal experience.

Action informed by both.

Somewhere in England, a woman may be sitting at her kitchen table with a letter from a solicitor and the conviction that her future is being taken away.

In our reconstructed case, the investigation has begun.

The documents remain to be examined.

The legal position remains uncertain.

There is no triumphant ending.

Not yet.
