# DOKOLOGY :: RIDE THE STORM

**The old world is ending. The new one hasn't loaded.**

`>> boot.void / centralcontroller.online`

You're worried about your job.

Maybe your business.

Maybe everything you've spent twenty years building.

AI is coming through the walls. The economic certainties are dissolving. Technologies nobody understands are appearing almost daily. The future is looking increasingly like a science-fiction novel written by someone on a heroic dose of LSD.

And yet, on Sunday, you're at a barbecue.

The sausages are sizzling. Someone's talking about their new kitchen.

Monday morning, you're taking the kids to school.

Or riding the Tube into London, scrolling through your phone, watching another piece of the future arrive without asking permission.

Maybe you work in technology, advertising, finance, PR.

Maybe you're a writer, an artist, a small business owner.

Maybe you live alone and spend your evenings wondering where the hell all this is going.

**I don't know either.**

Nobody really does.

But here's something I've learned.

You don't need to know what's coming to prepare yourself to meet it.

You need a different way of operating.

**Welcome to Dokology.**

## THE APOCALYPSE IS AN OPPORTUNITY

Not necessarily the end of civilisation.

Something stranger.

The end of one arrangement of civilisation.

Jobs, institutions, professions, reputations, identities. Things that once appeared permanent may become temporary.

Whole industries might be rebuilt.

The new machines won't simply give us more convenient ways of doing yesterday's work.

They may change what work actually means.

Some people will respond with terror.

Others will pretend nothing is happening.

And a few will pick up the controller.

They'll learn the new interfaces.

Explore the unfamiliar landscapes.

Find new ways to create, build, earn, think and live.

Not because they possess some secret knowledge of the future.

**Because they've learned how to navigate uncertainty.**

That's where Dokology begins.

## PICK UP THE CONTROLLER

Imagine you've been dropped into an enormous, unfamiliar game.

The landscape is shifting.

The rules are changing.

Some of the other players haven't realised the old game is over.

You've got a controller in your hands.

But nobody gave you the instructions.

Dokology is about discovering what the buttons do.

And, where possible, creating new ones.

Not by following another guru.

Not by collecting motivational slogans.

Not by hoping the universe will sort everything out.

But through observation, experimentation, technology, deliberate action and a radically different relationship with your own mind.

There will be mistakes.

There will be spectacular failures.

There may also be opportunities you couldn't have imagined six months earlier.

**You learn to move.**

## CENTRALCONTROLLER :: TAKE COMMAND

`>> centralcontroller.activate`

Your first task isn't to master artificial intelligence.

It's to pay attention to your own intelligence.

Most people spend their lives running on automatic.

Thoughts arrive. Emotions fire. Old memories seize the controls. A headline generates panic. An unexpected email ruins the afternoon.

The mind becomes a machine operated by everything except its owner.

Dokology begins with self-observation.

You learn to notice your thoughts without automatically obeying them.

Interrupt destructive internal dialogue.

Recognise when fear, excitement, habit or wishful thinking is distorting your decisions.

You develop what I call the **CentralController**.

Not a little dictator in your skull.

A practical position of awareness from which you can observe what's happening and choose your next move.

Think of it as a new control layer.

A way to stop being dragged through the landscape and start navigating it.

The first upgrade is internal.

Everything else builds upon it.

## BECOME AN AGENTIC ENGINEER

`>> dream.it.at.midnight`

`>> ship.it.by.dawn`

This is one of the great possibilities of MachineKind.

You can increasingly turn an idea into a working experiment with astonishing speed.

A website.

A book.

A piece of music.

A new business proposition.

An audio production.

A prototype.

A creative world that didn't exist yesterday.

You don't need to be a programmer.

Although knowing some code won't hurt.

You need to learn how to think in systems, issue clear instructions, evaluate results, correct mistakes and make decisions.

AI agents and other tools can become part of your working environment.

But they don't automatically know what you want.

And they shouldn't be running your life.

**You are the operator.**

The machines are part of the apparatus.

Together we explore how to use these emerging capabilities for your own projects, livelihood and creative ambitions.

The aim isn't to spend your days worshipping artificial intelligence.

It's to discover what becomes possible when you stop assuming yesterday's limitations still apply.

## DOOMJOY :: THE ART OF RIDING THE STORM

There's a peculiar exhilaration in all this.

I call it **DoomJoy**.

The recognition that enormous changes may be coming, combined with an almost unreasonable appetite for exploring them.

Not denying danger.

Not pretending there won't be casualties.

Not celebrating other people's misfortunes.

But refusing to let fear become your entire worldview.

DoomJoy is the decision to remain curious when the landscape turns strange.

To experiment when the old instructions stop working.

To find energy in uncertainty rather than being permanently paralysed by it.

There's an old punk attitude somewhere in here.

1977.

You don't have the right equipment.

You don't know whether anybody will listen.

You haven't even worked out all the chords.

**You form the band anyway.**

That's the spirit.

Only now the rehearsal room might contain a laptop, an AI model, a few lines of code, and an idea that would once have required a whole company to execute.

Make some noise.

See where it goes.

## THE DOKOLOGY OPERATING SYSTEM

This isn't a fixed course.

There are no compulsory modules to tick off, no motivational certificates, and no promises of miraculous transformation.

Dokology is a private working relationship.

We investigate your actual circumstances and develop practical methods for navigating them.

The framework includes:

### 01 :: CENTRALCONTROLLER
**Attention. Awareness. Command.**

Self-observation, mental silence, emotional regulation and the ability to interrupt automatic reactions.

Learn to distinguish the event from the story your mind is telling about it.

### 02 :: FIELD / SIGNAL / NOISE
**Read the landscape.**

Situational awareness, pattern recognition, strategic observation and separating useful information from distraction.

Understand the forces affecting your work, relationships, decisions and opportunities.

### 03 :: PROBABILITY NAVIGATION
**Change your position. Change your possibilities.**

Scenario thinking, decision engineering, timing, risk assessment and small experiments that can create larger opportunities.

No supernatural guarantees. No magic bullets.

Just a more deliberate relationship with possibility.

### 04 :: MACHINEKIND
**Work with the new intelligence.**

Explore AI, automation, agentic workflows, creative systems and emerging tools relevant to your circumstances.

You don't need to become a software engineer.

You do need to understand the changing environment.

### 05 :: AGENTIC ENGINEERING
**From intention to execution.**

Turn vague ambitions into clear objectives.

Build workflows.

Prototype ideas.

Test the results.

Learn when to delegate to machines and when to retain direct human control.

### 06 :: DOOMJOY
**Move through the unfamiliar.**

Develop adaptability, curiosity, resilience and creative initiative.

Learn to operate without requiring the world to become predictable first.

### 07 :: BOOT.VOID
**Drop the obsolete programming.**

Examine inherited assumptions, stale identities, repetitive habits and the internal narratives that keep you running yesterday's software.

Keep what works.

Discard what doesn't.

Build something better.

## AND WHAT ABOUT MAGICK?

I've spent decades exploring unusual systems of thought.

Chaos magick. Ritual. Folklore. Altered states. Dreams. Symbolism. The stranger territories of consciousness.

I'm not throwing any of that away.

But Dokology doesn't require you to believe in the occult.

You can approach these things as symbolic technologies, creative exercises, experiments in attention, or simply unfamiliar ways of looking at a problem.

A ritual can be treated as an exercise in commitment and focused intention.

A sigil as a symbolic encoding of an objective.

A dream as material for reflection and creative investigation.

Whether there's anything beyond the psychological mechanisms is a separate question.

An interesting one.

But you don't have to settle it to begin exploring.

**What matters is what you observe, what you learn, and what you actually do.**

## THIS IS NOT LIFE COACHING AS USUAL

I'm not here to tell you the universe has a special plan.

I'm not here to promise you'll become rich, enlightened or immune to the consequences of technological disruption.

And I'm certainly not going to pretend I possess a secret map of the future.

My work is to help you develop your own navigational intelligence.

We can examine business problems, creative projects, difficult decisions, relationships, personal transitions, emerging technologies and the assumptions shaping your life.

I'll challenge your thinking where necessary.

I'll help you notice opportunities and risks.

We'll devise experiments, develop protocols, review results and adjust course.

My background includes journalism, writing, publishing, entrepreneurship, decades of independent exploration and a longstanding fascination with consciousness and the unexplained.

I've spent much of my life working outside conventional systems.

Now the conventional systems themselves are changing.

That makes this a particularly interesting time to be alive.

## WHO IS DOKOLOGY FOR?

For the entrepreneur watching an established business model begin to fracture.

For the creative who suspects the machines might become extraordinary collaborators.

For the professional wondering what their skills will be worth in five years.

For the person in transition who knows the old arrangements aren't working but hasn't yet discovered the next move.

For people fascinated by consciousness, technology, autonomy and the unexplored possibilities of human experience.

And for those who look at the gathering storm and feel something besides fear.

A little electricity.

A peculiar excitement.

The thought:

**What if I could learn to ride this thing?**

You don't have to be fearless.

You don't have to be technical.

You don't have to share my worldview.

But you do need curiosity, initiative and a willingness to test unfamiliar approaches.

Dokology isn't passive reassurance.

It's participation.

## HOW WE WORK

The starting point is your life as it exists now.

Not an idealised version.

Not the person you think you're supposed to become.

The actual problems, ambitions, uncertainties and possibilities in front of you.

We identify what matters.

Examine what's changing.

Develop your operating framework.

Then begin putting it to work.

Monthly private sessions provide the main structure.

Between sessions, we use secure communication for agreed support, updates and reviewing relevant developments.

Depending on your circumstances, I may create bespoke written protocols, audio exercises, decision maps, practical assignments or AI-assisted workflows.

Some months may be focused on immediate problems.

Others on building something entirely new.

The continuity is important.

Dokology is about developing capabilities that become increasingly useful over time.

**The ultimate aim is greater autonomy. Not dependence on me.**

## DREAM IT AT MIDNIGHT. SHIP IT BY DAWN.

This isn't a promise that every idea can be realised overnight.

It's a change in orientation.

Instead of spending six months wondering whether something might work, we look for the smallest useful experiment.

Instead of waiting for the perfect moment, we learn when an imperfect launch is worth making.

Instead of being intimidated by technologies we haven't mastered, we begin investigating them.

You could arrive with a business problem.

Or a creative ambition.

Or simply the unsettling sense that your old life is becoming obsolete.

We find a starting point.

We build.

We observe.

We adapt.

And we go again.
